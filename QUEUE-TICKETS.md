# QUEUE-TICKETS.md — the number on the card in the patient's hand
### v1.0 · 12 September 2026 · Owner: Carl. A plan, not yet built. Affects `/practice/tablet` (existing) and `/practice/frontdesk` (planned, see `FRONT-DESK-PAGE.md`). Hard rules from CLAUDE.md §2 apply unchanged.

their queue_id — why? because some practices have a manual card system. The
patient on going to the practice, takes a card with a number and reception will

automated system via PMS + AoBPlatform, or Manual Card + AoBPlatform, or just

---

## 1. What this actually buys

Three ways a practice can run the front desk, and the ticket is what lets the
middle one work at all:

| Mode | How the patient is identified in the room | What the ticket does |
|---|---|---|
| PMS + AoBPlatform | The practice software says who arrived | Optional. A convenience for calling people up |
| **Manual card + AoBPlatform** | A paper card with a number | **Essential.** It is the only handle reception has on "who is next" |
| AoBPlatform alone | Reception types the patient in | Optional, same as the first |

A practice that has not bought or wired a patient-management feed is currently
asked to match names on a screen against faces in a room. The card number is how
that room already works, and the platform should speak it rather than replace
it.

## 2. The one thing it must never become

**A ticket is not an identity identifier, and nothing may verify a patient by
it.** The approved set is name, date of birth, gender, address, patient record
number and IHI, it is non-configurable, and the whole regime's most likely
design mistake is quietly adding a seventh (hard rule 1, REQ-VER-02).

A card number is easy to hand to the wrong person, is reused every day, and is
often lying face up on a counter. So:

- The kiosk never accepts it as one of the three identifiers a walk-up types.
- No verification path reads it, and no verification log records it.
- It never appears on the agreement artefact. The s 65C data set is fixed;
  adding a field to a signed instrument is the risk hard rule 4 exists to
  prevent, and this one is not even a particular of the agreement.

It is a label for a person's place in a queue on one day. That is all it is, and
the tests should say so by name.

## 3. Where it lives

**On the arrival, not the agreement and not the tablet session.**

- *Not the agreement*: it is not a particular, must not be rendered, and would
  then be caught up in supersession, which would be absurd — replacing who signs
  does not hand the patient a new card.
- *Not the tablet session*: those are created on push and ended on signature.
  The number exists from the moment the patient walks in, before any push, and
  survives a recall and a re-send.
- *The arrival* is exactly "this person turned up at this practice at this
  time", which is the same scope as the card. `Arrival` already carries
  `practiceId`, `arrivedAt` and the link to the agreement it opened.

**And the desk already knows how to find it.** The queue is ordered by walking
each agreement back through `supersedesAgreementId` to the arrival that started
it (`readPushableRows`, 11 Sep 2026). The ticket is read on exactly that path,
so a corrected or superseded row keeps its number for free.

Shape: a short string, not an integer — practices use `A12` and `012` as well as
`12`. Trimmed, upper-cased, maximum eight characters, and stored as typed
otherwise.

**Three sources, in precedence order (Carl, 12 September 2026;
D-2026-09-12-18).** This paragraph used to read *"never generated by us"*. It is
now narrowed rather than kept:

1. **The practice's own card**, where they hand them out.
2. **A number from the practice's software**, where a feed carries one.
3. **One we allocate**, where there is neither.

The original worry was a SECOND queue competing with the practice's own, and it
still holds — which is exactly what precedence protects. Where they have a card
box we never invent a rival to it; where they have neither cards nor a feed
there is no other queue, so ours is not a competitor but the only handle the
patient has. **The source is recorded with the number**, because "A12, off their
card box" and "we made this up" are different facts.

## 4. Only where a practice uses cards

A practice setting, `usesQueueTickets`, default off. When off, no field appears
anywhere — an extra box on a page whose whole purpose is being learnable on day
one is precisely the wrong direction (`FRONT-DESK-PAGE.md` §4).

When on, the field appears in three places and nowhere else:

1. **The send step on `/practice/tablet`** — one small input beside the existing
   numbered strip, optional, not a fourth step and not a gate. Sending without a
   number must always work; care is never blocked (hard rule 8), and a receptionist
   whose card box has run out must not be stuck.
2. **The front desk page** — on the patient panel, and as the leading element of
   each row in the queue rail, because "call number twelve" is the job.
3. **Optionally on the kiosk's first screen** — "Number 12" under the patient's
   name, so the person holding the card can see it is theirs. Worth building; it
   is the cheapest possible confirmation that the right agreement is in front of
   the right person, and it shows nothing a bystander could not already read off
   the card.

## 5. Rules

1. **Optional, always.** No flow refuses to proceed for want of a ticket.
2. **Editable until the visit ends**, because cards get swapped and mistyped.
   Changing it is an ordinary correction, not a supersession — it changes nothing
   about the agreement.
3. **A duplicate warns, never blocks.** Two live visits with number 12 today is
   worth saying out loud, and is also perfectly possible when a card box wraps
   around. Say it beside the field; do not refuse the send.
4. **Practice-scoped and day-scoped.** A number means nothing outside its
   practice and its day, and the queries must be scoped so it cannot be used to
   find a person across either.
5. **Events record that it was set, not what it was.** `arrival.ticket_set`
   carrying ids and the fact — the house style for anything that identifies a
   person in a room (hard rule 9's spirit; the value is not needed to prove
   anything later).
6. **Never in a log, an error message or a URL.**

## 5a. The ticket is not the running order

Where a practice uses cards, it is tempting to treat the number as the queue.
It is not. The number is the PATIENT's handle — what they are holding and what
they will be called by — and the running order is RECEPTION's, changed through
the morning as walk-ins arrive (`FRONT-DESK-PAGE.md` §12.6).

Moving somebody up the list does **not** renumber their card, and cannot: they
are holding it. The desk shows both, and they will often disagree — number 14
being called before number 9 is an ordinary morning, not an error state, and
nothing on screen should suggest otherwise.

## 6. Named tests

- `a_queue_ticket_is_never_accepted_as_an_identity_identifier` — the kiosk's verification path refuses it outright.
- `a_queue_ticket_never_reaches_the_agreement_artefact` — rendered output compared, hard rule 4 and the fixed s 65C set.
- `sending_to_a_tablet_works_with_no_ticket` — hard rule 8.
- `a_superseded_row_keeps_its_queue_ticket` — it is read through the chain, like the arrival ordering.
- `a_duplicate_ticket_warns_and_still_sends`.
- `queue_tickets_are_invisible_when_the_practice_does_not_use_them`.
- `a_ticket_event_carries_ids_and_never_the_number`.
- `queue_tickets_do_not_cross_a_practice_boundary` — fails closed.
- `moving_a_patient_up_the_queue_does_not_change_their_ticket` — the card is in their hand.

## 8. For Carl to decide

- **Does the tablet show the number?** (§4.3.) Recommended yes; it is the cheapest confirmation that the right person has the right form.
- **Who may set it?** Anyone at the desk, presumably — but if a practice wants it locked after the push, that is a different rule and worth knowing now.
- **Does it need to appear on the practice's own reports later**, or is it purely an in-the-moment handle? That decides whether it is retained past the visit or cleared with it.
