# Telehealth and the Assignment of Benefit: what the government sources say

*Research note, 11 October 2026. Official sources only: legislation.gov.au, health.gov.au, mbsonline.gov.au (MBS Online), servicesaustralia.gov.au and the Medical Board of Australia. Every fact gives its URL and the date the page or document shows. "Not found" means I looked and could not find a source. Nothing here comes from memory. All wording is my own summary unless shown in quotation marks.*

---

## Summary: what matters most for the product

1. **Verbal assignment ends after 30 June 2027. That is confirmed.** The regulation made on 6 August 2026 (F2026L01041, taken to have commenced on 1 July 2026) lets an *episodic* agreement entered into **between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027** go without a signature and date if the assignor agrees verbally. In that case the agreement must say that verbal agreement was given, and on what date. An *enduring* agreement made on verbal agreement **stops having effect at the start of 1 July 2027**. From 1 July 2027 the law again requires a written agreement, signed and dated by the assignor (Health Insurance Regulations 2018, s 65C(6)(c)).

2. **Telehealth has no special AoB rules.** The Department's FAQ (as at 27 August 2026) says the AoB requirements for telehealth are the same as for face-to-face care. The agreement can be made before or after the consultation, as long as it is done before the claim. For telehealth the FAQ names three routes: an electronic signature through a portal, online form or practice system; a paper agreement completed and sent back electronically; or verbal assignment during the transition. Services Australia lists "an SMS link to a form plus a tick box" as one acceptable electronic method.

3. **The particulars must reach the assignor in writing *before or when* the agreement is offered** (s 65C(4)). For a GP post-service agreement they are:
   - the patient's name;
   - the proposed date of the agreement;
   - that it is a post-agreement;
   - the provider's name and the address of the place of practice they are working from at the time of the service, **or** the provider number for that place;
   - the date of the service;
   - the **MBS item number**.

   The agreement must also say whether the assignor is the patient, and it must be signed and dated (or carry the verbal-agreement statement and date). Carl's flow (consent to the telehealth consultation first, then sign the DB4-equivalent on the phone afterwards) fits the post-agreement shape. That holds only if the step before the consultation is **not** worded or recorded as an offer to assign. A post-agreement is defined as one where neither party offers to enter it until after the service (s 65C(3)(b)(i)). If the step before the consultation reads as an offer to assign, it could become a pre-agreement, which needs different particulars. **This is the biggest open design question. Ask the Department (see the questions section).**

4. **The electronic signature test is Electronic Transactions Act 1999 s 10.** The method must identify the person and show their intention. It must also be either "as reliable as appropriate" for the purpose or shown in fact to have done both jobs. For an SMS "YES" reply, the Department's FAQ says the answer is "Potentially". It depends on whether the practice can show four things: who responded, what they were shown, that the reply clearly agreed, and that a reliable, auditable record was kept. **No government source sets out a specific identity-check method for remote assignment.**

5. **Retention.** The Act (s 127A) says AoB records must be kept **in electronic form** (or another form the Secretary allows). They must be kept until the latest of three dates: 2 years after the agreement stops being in force, 2 years after the record was made, or any date set by the regulations. Guidance pages describe the period differently: "2 years from the date the claim is made" (Services Australia AoB page and Department FAQ) and "2 years from the date the agreement is made" (Services Australia manual-AoB page). Building to the statute covers both versions. **Copy for the assignor:** the professional must give a copy of the terms if asked, as soon as practicable (s 127(1), civil penalty of 5 penalty units). One line in the Department FAQ goes further and says a copy must be provided before the claim. The others say "on request".

6. **The AoB does not have to say it was a telehealth service.** Nothing in s 65C asks for it. For a post-service agreement, the item number already shows video or phone, because telehealth items have their own numbers. In the *clinical record*, any exemption from the telehealth eligibility rule **must** be documented at the time of service. The Medical Board's guideline also asks doctors to record the type of technology used.

7. **Where the practitioner sits.** The MBS GP telehealth factsheet (23 October 2025) says providers do not need to be in their practice. They should use the provider number for the appropriate practice. The claim particulars rule (reg 51(2)) and the AoB identifying details (s 65C(5)) both allow a provider number for "any place of practice" where the service is not given at a place of practice the provider has a number for. Location Specific Practice Numbers apply only to diagnostic imaging and radiation oncology. Both patient and provider must be physically in Australia.

8. **MyMedicare matters for telehealth in three ways:**
   - it is an alternative eligibility pathway (since 1 November 2025);
   - longer *phone* consultations (Levels C and D) are available only to patients registered with the billing practice;
   - enduring AoB agreements for GP services are tied to the MyMedicare practice, with services rendered "at or from" that location. **Not confirmed:** whether a GP consulting by telehealth from home counts as "from" the agreement location.

## 1. Assigning the benefit when the patient is not physically present

**The law.**
- Health Insurance Act s 20A(1) allows an eligible person (the *assignor*) to assign the right to the Medicare benefit to the professional, provided any requirements in the regulations are met. *(Act, compilation 19 September 2026.)*
- Section 65B of the Regulations says a bulk-billing assignment must be either an **episodic** agreement that meets s 65C or an **enduring** agreement that meets s 65CB. *(HI Regulations, compilation 1 July 2026.)*
- Section 65C(3) sets out the two episodic types:
  - a **pre-agreement**, made before the service, which can cover services over up to 6 months;
  - a **post-agreement**, which neither party offers until after the service.
- Section 65C(4): the professional must make sure the assignor is given the listed information **in writing, before or at the time** either party offers the agreement.
- Section 65C(6): the agreement must contain that information, say whether the assignor is the person receiving the service, and be a written agreement **signed and dated by the assignor**.

**Verbal agreement and its end date. Confirmed.**
- F2026L01041 added ss 65C(7)–(8). An episodic agreement entered into between **1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027** need not be signed and dated if the assignor agrees verbally. The agreement must then state that verbal agreement was given and the date it was given. *(Regulation made 6 August 2026, commenced 1 July 2026.)*
- Matching changes for enduring agreements: s 65CB(6)–(8) (verbal agreement and verbal declarations), and a new s 65CA(8)(f). Under s 65CA(8)(f), an enduring agreement made on verbal agreement stops having effect **at the start of 1 July 2027**. *(Same instrument.)*
- Services Australia describes the transition as 12 months from 1 July 2026, with verbal assignment allowed "for all bulk billed services". With verbal consent the provider must do three things:
  - explain how the verbal agreement will be recorded, and confirm the patient agrees;
  - record that they agreed verbally;
  - keep the record for 2 years.

  If a Services Australia form is used, type "assignor verbally agreed" in the assignor signature field. *(SA AoB page, last updated 2 October 2026.)*
- The Department says verbal assignment is a "12-month temporary provision until 30 June 2027". *(DoH provider page, 28 August 2026.)*

**How the assignment may be made: electronic methods, SMS and email.**
- Services Australia's examples of electronic assignment *(SA AoB page, 2 October 2026)*:
  - the particulars are emailed and the assignor replies agreeing;
  - the particulars are built into booking software and the patient agrees when booking;
  - the practice texts a **link to a form** containing the particulars, and the assignor accepts by **ticking a box**.
- On telehealth, the Department's FAQ says the same AoB requirements apply as for face-to-face care. Assignment may come before or after the consultation if it is done before the claim. The routes it lists are an electronic signature through a patient portal, online form or practice management system; a paper agreement completed and returned electronically; or verbal assignment during the transition. For a post-service assignment, the agreement must reflect the service actually given, including the MBS item number(s). Practices should make sure telehealth workflows can create, complete, store and retrieve AoB records the same way as for face-to-face care. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026, section 7.)*
- Medicare Easyclaim: the FAQ says Easyclaim remains a valid way to get an AoB in general. However, Services Australia's telehealth claiming page says Easyclaim **cannot be used to bulk bill telehealth items**. *(SA "Submit a telehealth claim", last updated 20 March 2025.)*

**What must be recorded (GP telehealth, episodic post-agreement).** Section 65C(4)–(6), plus table item 6 and s 65C(5):
- the name of the person who received the service;
- the date the agreement is proposed to be entered into;
- that it is a post-agreement;
- the professional's identifying details: **either** their name and the address of the place of practice they are working from at the time of the service, **or** the provider number for that place of practice (or, if that does not apply, for any place of practice);
- the date the service was rendered;
- the MBS item number;
- whether the assignor is the patient;
- the assignor's signature and date, **or**, until 30 June 2027, a statement that verbal agreement was given and its date.

The assignor's *name* is not needed for an episodic agreement. It is needed for an enduring agreement. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026, "Is the assignor's name required?")*

For an episodic pre-agreement (table item 5), the "category of professional services" from the *Health Insurance (Assignment of Medicare Benefits – Categories of Professional Services) Determination 2026* replaces the item number. The *date the service will be rendered* replaces the date it was rendered.

**No changes after signing.** The FAQ says an agreement should be completed accurately *before* it is signed or agreed to. If the assignor, the practitioner or the services change, a new agreement is needed. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)*

**Who keeps it, and for how long.**
- Act s 127A(1) and (4): the professional must keep the records the regulations specify about the assignment. They must be kept **in electronic form**, or another form approved by the Secretary.
- s 127A(5): records must be kept until the **latest** of: 2 years after the agreement stops being in force; 2 years after the record was created; or a date set by the regulations. Failing to keep them carries a civil penalty of 5 penalty units (s 127A(6)). *(Act, compilation 19 September 2026.)*
- The guidance puts the period differently: "2 years from the date the claim is made" (SA AoB page, 2 October 2026; DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026) and "2 years from the date the agreement is made" (SA manual-AoB page, 1 July 2026).
- Responsibility: records may be stored by the practice or a software provider, but responsibility rests with **the practitioner who claims the benefit**. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026, section 10.)*
- Completed agreements are not sent to Services Australia, except to support manually lodged claims. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026; SA AoB page, 2 October 2026.)*
- Security: no particular platform is required. Records must be stored securely, stay complete and legible, be retrievable, and be handled in line with the Privacy Act 1988. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)*

**Copy to the assignor.**
- Act s 127(1): if the assignor asks, the professional must give them a copy of the terms of the agreement **as soon as practicable**. Civil penalty: 5 penalty units. *(Act, compilation 19 September 2026.)*
- Services Australia says patients no longer have to keep a copy, but the practice must give one if asked. *(SA AoB page, 2 October 2026.)*
- **Inconsistency.** One passage of the Department FAQ (section 4, episodic agreements) says the patient or assignor **must be provided with a copy of the completed agreement** before the claim and asked to sign it. Elsewhere the same FAQ says "if requested". The statute requires the written particulars before or at the offer (s 65C(4)) and a copy on request (s 127(1)). *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)*
- The superseded 2024 MBS factsheet said the patient must get a completed copy electronically. It has been replaced by the 1 July 2026 rules but is still linked from MBS Online. *(MBS factsheet, 4 July 2024.)*

## 2. The Electronic Transactions Act test, and identity checks

**The statutory test.** Electronic Transactions Act 1999 s 10(1): where a Commonwealth law requires a person's signature, an electronic communication meets that requirement if all of the following apply:
- (a) a method is used to identify the person and to indicate the person's intention about the information communicated;
- (b) the method was either "as reliable as appropriate" for the purpose in all the circumstances, or proven in fact to have done (a);
- (d) where the signature is given to someone who is not a Commonwealth entity (here, the practitioner), that person consents to the method being used.

*(ETA, compilation 5 December 2025.)*

**How the Department applies it to AoB.** *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026, "What is an acceptable signature?" and "Is sending a text message and receiving a reply of YES sufficient?")*
- A valid electronic signature must identify the assignor, show they agree to assign, and comply with privacy obligations and ETA s 10.
- The ETA does not prescribe a type of signature. Examples given: signing on a tablet or touchscreen, typing a name into an electronic form, ticking an "I accept" box, or using a secure digital signature platform.
- Practices should make sure the chosen method "can reliably identify the assignor" and clearly records their agreement.
- SMS "YES": "Potentially" acceptable. It depends on whether the practitioner can show who responded, what information they were given before responding, that the reply clearly agreed, and that a reliable, auditable record was kept.
- The Department points to the Attorney-General's Department for more guidance on electronic signatures, and suggests independent legal advice where needed.
- Services Australia makes the same points: SMS-link-and-tick-box and email-reply are both listed as acceptable examples. *(SA AoB page, 2 October 2026.)*

**Identity checks for remote assignment: not found.** No Services Australia or Department page reviewed gives a specific identity-verification method (such as which identifiers to check, or one-time codes) for AoB made remotely. The requirement stays at the general level: the method must reliably identify the assignor.

The closest professional-standards rule is the Medical Board's telehealth guideline. Doctors should confirm, to the best of their ability, the identity of the patient and anyone else present **at each consultation**. *(Medical Board, page reviewed 1 September 2023.)* That rule is about the clinical consultation, not the assignment.

**Who may be the assignor (relevant to remote flows).** *(SA AoB page, 2 October 2026; DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)*
- The assignor is the person who would otherwise have incurred the expense and been entitled to the benefit. That is not always the patient.
- Treating practitioners and their staff should not act as assignor.
- Generally a child of 14 or over can make their own agreement.
- For enduring agreements, the Regulations (s 65CA(2)–(3)) require the assignor to be at least 14. They list who counts as a "responsible person" (for a relative, at least 18 and a member of the household).

## 3. Eligibility for GP telehealth items

**The rule.** Medical practitioners billing non-referred telehealth items (Group A40 Subgroups 1, 2, 11, 13, 15, 16, 17, 19 and 20) may provide a telehealth service only if one of these applies:
- the patient is **registered in MyMedicare** and the service comes from that registered practice; **or**
- the practitioner is the patient's **eligible telehealth practitioner**; **or**
- an exemption applies.

*(MBS note AN.1.1, read 11 October 2026; AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025.)*

**The "eligible telehealth practitioner" test.** The old name, "established clinical relationship", was replaced from 1 November 2025. One of these must be true:
- the practitioner gave the patient a face-to-face, MBS-billed service in the last 12 months; or
- the practitioner works at a practice where the patient had a face-to-face, MBS-billed service arranged by that practice in the last 12 months (given by another doctor or nurse practitioner there, or by a practice nurse or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health worker or practitioner on a doctor's behalf); or
- the practitioner is in the Approved Medical Deputising Service (AMDS) program, and the AMDS provider has a formal agreement with a practice that gave the patient a face-to-face service in the last 12 months.

A previous video or phone consultation does **not** count as face-to-face. The test is rolling. *(AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025; GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*

**MyMedicare.** It is an *alternative pathway*, not an exemption. From 1 November 2025, registered patients can get these services from their MyMedicare practice even if they have not attended in the past 12 months. *(AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025.)*

**Exemptions: each one in Carl's list checked.**

| Claimed exemption | Verified? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MyMedicare-registered patients | **Partly correct.** It is a separate eligibility pathway, limited to telehealth from the patient's *registered practice*. It is not an exemption that applies at any practice | AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025 |
| Children under 12 months | **Yes** | AN.1.1; SA "Who can claim" (7 July 2026) |
| People experiencing homelessness | **Yes.** Homeless means the current living arrangement is inadequate, has no tenure (or only short, non-extendable tenure), or gives no control of, and access to, space for social relations | AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory |
| Urgent after-hours | **Yes.** Urgent after-hours service in unsociable hours (Subgroup 29; video items 92210 GP, 92211 other medical practitioner) | AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory; item 92210 |
| Blood-borne virus, sexual or reproductive health | **Yes.** Applies to the specific items in Subgroups 39 (video) and 40 (phone). Assisted reproductive technology and antenatal services are excluded | AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory; SA "Who can claim" |
| Natural disaster areas | **Yes.** The patient must be in a local government area that a State or Territory has declared a natural disaster area, while the declaration lasts. Confirming and documenting this is advised | AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory |

**Other exemptions found.** *(AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025; SA "Who can claim", 7 July 2026)*
- services by a medical practitioner at an Aboriginal Medical Service or ACCHS;
- people isolating or quarantined under a COVID-19 State or Territory public health order (the advisory says this provision is idle until a jurisdiction uses it);
- specified mental health items (focussed psychological strategies, Subgroups 3 and 10);
- eating disorder planning and treatment (Subgroups 21 and 25–28);
- preparing or reviewing a GP chronic condition management plan (Subgroup 13). These are subject to the separate MyMedicare or usual-medical-practitioner rule in AN.0.47;
- named urgent care clinics (Broome WA and Devonport Tas, using listed items), and services under the Commonwealth Urgent Care Clinic Program.

**Not exempt** *(AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025)*:
- mental health treatment plan telehealth items (since 1 November 2025);
- specific provider types or new practices: "no exemptions" for either;
- the advisory's scenario table lists as not exempt: patients awaiting a COVID-19 test result, patients with a chronic condition or who are immunocompromised, and non-directive pregnancy counselling. *(The text extracted from that table was garbled, so check this row against the PDF.)*
- telehealth services under these rules are not available to admitted hospital patients, or where the patient or provider is overseas.

**Documenting exemptions.** Recording the exemption relied on, in the clinical notes at the time of service, is described as a "legislated requirement". AN.1.1 adds that the clinical reasoning must also be recorded. *(AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025; AN.1.1.)*

**Changes since November 2025.**
- The 1 November 2025 changes themselves: the MyMedicare pathway; mental health treatment plan telehealth items lost their exemption; the eligibility rule extended to nurse practitioners. *(GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*
- No later change to the GP eligibility rule was found. The newest Services Australia statement (7 July 2026) matches AN.1.1.
- From **1 November 2026**, four new phone items for chronic condition management plans arrive (92033, 92034, 92063, 92064). The six inpatient telehealth psychiatry items become permanent. *(MBS November 2026 news, updated 1 October 2026.)*
- From 1 March 2026, new patient-end support items let a GP, prescribed medical practitioner or nurse practitioner support a patient face to face during a specialist video consultation. *(MBS March 2026 news, updated 2 March 2026.)*

## 4. Phone versus video

**General position.**
- Video is the preferred substitute for face-to-face care. Phone may be used where clinically appropriate and an MBS item exists. Video and phone have **separate item numbers**.
- Telehealth items carry the same clinical requirements and the same benefit as the matching face-to-face items.
- Telehealth items cannot be used for email consultations, or for admitted or hospital-in-the-home patients (with some psychiatry exceptions).

*(SA MBS telehealth items page, 1 November 2025; GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*

**GP general attendance structure** *(AN.0.74 table; GP factsheet, 23 October 2025)*:

| Level | Video | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| A: straightforward | 91790 | 91890 (short, under 6 minutes) |
| B: 6 to 20 minutes | 91800 | 91891 (6 minutes or more) |
| C: 20 minutes or more | 91801 | 91900: **MyMedicare-registered patients of the billing practice only** |
| D: 40 minutes or more | 91802 | 91910: **MyMedicare only** |
| E: 60 minutes or more | 91920 | **none** (video only) |

- The MBS factsheet says plainly that longer phone services (20 and 40 minutes) are only for patients registered in MyMedicare. *(GP factsheet, 23 October 2025; item 91900, read 11 October 2026.)*
- Telehealth attendance items are **not split into business-hours and after-hours versions** in the table. The only after-hours-specific telehealth items are the **video-only** urgent unsociable-hours items 92210 and 92211 (Subgroup 29). No phone equivalent was found. *(AN.0.74; item 92210.)*

**Other GP items that are video only (no phone item)** *(SA MBS telehealth items page, 1 November 2025)*:
- mental health treatment plan preparation (92112–92113, 92116–92117, and the prescribed medical practitioner equivalents);
- eating disorder plan preparation (92146–92149, 92150–92153);
- GP chronic condition management plan preparation and review (92029, 92030; prescribed medical practitioner 92060, 92061), until the new phone items arrive on 1 November 2026;
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health assessment (92004).

Focussed psychological strategies and eating disorder psychological treatment have both video and phone items.

**Where to look up items.** MBS Online telehealth hub: https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/Factsheet-Telehealth-Updates-April%202023. The GP factsheet (17 pages) has the full GP tables.

**Professional Services Review thresholds.** A practitioner who renders 80 or more relevant services on 20 or more days in 12 months is referred to the Director of PSR. Under the "30/20 rule", so is one who renders **30 or more phone services a day** on 20 or more days. *(GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*

## 5. Bulk billing incentives, BBPIP and MyMedicare

**Bulk Billing Practice Incentive Program (BBPIP), from 1 November 2025.**
- Participating practices get an extra **12.5%** on every dollar of MBS benefit from eligible services, split equally between the practice and the provider.
- The practice must be registered for **MyMedicare** and BBPIP, and must bulk bill all eligible services for all Medicare-eligible patients. *(SA About BBPIP, 3 December 2025; BBPIP guidelines, 3 March 2026.)*
- **Patients do not need to be MyMedicare-registered** for their services to count. *(BBPIP guidelines, 3 March 2026, s 2.1.8 and s 3.2.)*

**Telehealth counts.** The BBPIP eligible-services list (from 1 March 2026) includes:
- the video and phone general attendance items at Levels A–E (91790, 91800–91802, 91920, 91890, 91891 and related numbers);
- the MyMedicare phone items 91900 and 91910;
- BBVSRH telehealth items, chronic condition management video items, mental health telehealth items;
- the urgent after-hours video items 92210 and 92211.

*(BBPIP eligible services, from 1 March 2026.)*

**Triple bulk billing incentive and telehealth.** The triple incentive applies to:
- MBS Level B video and phone consultations;
- video and phone consultations longer than 20 minutes (Levels C, D and E, with E video only) **where the patient is registered with the practice through MyMedicare**.

The standard single incentive applies to Level C, D and E telehealth where the patient is *not* in MyMedicare. The FAQ says these settings did not change from 1 November 2025. *(BBPIP FAQ, 7 April 2026.)*

**Other.** Telehealth services count towards SWPE calculations for PIP and WIP payments. *(GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*

**MyMedicare's role, in summary.** It gives:
- the alternative telehealth eligibility pathway;
- sole access to longer phone consultations;
- the triple incentive on longer telehealth consultations;
- the practice-level requirement for BBPIP;
- the basis for GP enduring AoB agreements.

On the last point, an enduring agreement is per practitioner. It covers only services rendered "at or from" the MyMedicare practice, by a GP registered with MyMedicare at that practice whose provider number identifies the practice (s 65CA(5)). The FAQ lists "telehealth consultations" among services an enduring agreement can cover for MyMedicare patients. *(HI Regulations, compilation 1 July 2026; DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)* Post-service notifications are required for MyMedicare enduring agreements, within 24 hours of the claim. *(DoH FAQ, 27 August 2026.)*

## 6. After-hours telehealth and the practitioner's location

**The practitioner does not need to be at the practice.** "Providers do not need to be in their practice to provide telehealth services." They should use the provider number for the appropriate practice and provide safe services to normal professional standards. *(GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)*

**Location particulars on the claim.** Reg 51(2) of the HI Regulations sets the particulars of the person rendering a general medical service. It must be one of:
- (a) their name and the address of the place of practice where the service was rendered; or
- (b) the provider number, if the service was rendered at a place of practice they hold a number for; or
- (c) if not rendered at such a place, the provider number for **any** place where they practise.

It must also include a statement that the service was provided by that person. *(HI Regulations, compilation 1 July 2026.)*

**Location particulars on the assignment.** Section 65C(5) uses the same structure for a post-agreement:
- the professional's name and the address of the place of practice they are working *from* at the time of the service; or
- the provider number for that place; or
- if that does not apply, the provider number for any place of practice.

*(HI Regulations, compilation 1 July 2026.)*

**Location Specific Practice Numbers** apply to diagnostic imaging and radiation oncology premises (reg 53; Services Australia LSPN line), **not** to GP telehealth. *(HI Regulations; SA contacts page, 30 September 2026.)*

**Practice location IDs and BBPIP.** BBPIP is assessed per practice location (Organisation Site). Locums need a provider number for each BBPIP location. Each eligible GP service delivered at a participating practice's physical location must be bulk billed. *(BBPIP guidelines, 3 March 2026; BBPIP FAQ, 7 April 2026.)* No rule was found on how a home-based telehealth service is attributed to a BBPIP location beyond "use the provider number for the appropriate practice".

**Both parties must be in Australia.** Medicare benefits are paid only for services performed in Australia, and "Both the provider and the patient must be physically located within Australia, including for telehealth." *(DoH, 19 December 2025.)*

**After-hours.** The urgent after-hours video items need four things: unsociable hours; a request from the patient or a responsible person in the same unbroken after-hours period; a condition that needs urgent assessment; and one patient on one occasion. These services are exempt from the eligible telehealth practitioner rule, including when an AMDS provides them. *(Item 92210; GP factsheet, 23 October 2025.)* The definition of "unsociable hours" was **not fetched** (see the unconfirmed list).

## 7. Must the record or the assignment say "telehealth"?

- **The assignment: no explicit requirement found.** Sections 65C and 65CB list the required particulars and none of them is "mode of delivery". For a post-agreement, the required MBS item number already identifies video or phone. A pre-agreement uses the service *category* from the 2026 Categories Determination; whether that category separates telehealth was **not checked**. *(HI Regulations, compilation 1 July 2026.)*
- **The claim: no explicit telehealth flag found.** Reg 50(1) requires a description sufficient to identify the item. The telehealth item number does that. *(HI Regulations.)*
- **The clinical record:**
  - Any telehealth eligibility exemption, and the clinical reasoning, must be documented in the clinical notes at the time of service. *(AN.1.1; AskMBS advisory, 1 November 2025.)*
  - The general standard of adequate and contemporaneous records applies (GN.15.39): a separate dated entry for each attendance, with enough clinical information to explain the type of service. *(GN.15.39.)*
  - The Medical Board's guideline says doctors should also record the type of technology used during a telehealth consultation. *(Medical Board, page reviewed 1 September 2023.)*

## What we could not confirm

1. **Identity-check methods for remote assignment.** No Services Australia or Department source sets out how to verify the assignor's identity for an SMS or email AoB beyond the general ETA test ("reliably identify").
2. **Whether a step before the consultation that confirms consent to the telehealth consultation turns the later signature into a pre-agreement.** This depends on whether that step counts as an "offer" to enter the agreement (s 65C(3)(b)(i)). No guidance found.
3. **Retention start point.** The Act sets the latest of: 2 years after the agreement stops being in force, or 2 years after creation. Guidance says variously "2 years from the date the claim is made" and "from the date the agreement is made". Which date compliance staff will apply is not confirmed.
4. **When the copy must be given.** The statute requires a copy on request. One FAQ passage says a copy must be provided before the claim and the patient asked to sign. Not reconciled.
5. **Whether a GP's telehealth service from home counts as "at or from" the MyMedicare agreement location** for an enduring agreement (s 65CA(5)(d)).
6. **Whether Easyclaim still cannot be used to bulk bill telehealth.** The Services Australia page saying so is dated 20 March 2025, before the 1 July 2026 changes. The FAQ says Easyclaim is valid for AoB generally.
7. **The current status of the "digital approved form" for telehealth AoB** on the Health Systems Developer Portal, now that an approved form is no longer required. Not found.
8. **The definition of "unsociable hours"** for items 92210 and 92211 (not fetched).
9. **Whether the pre-agreement "category of professional services"** (Categories Determination 2026) separates telehealth from face-to-face. Not checked.
10. **The episodic data-element table in the Department FAQ.** It is an image and its text could not be extracted. The Regulations were used instead.
11. **The scenario table in the AskMBS advisory** (which situations are or are not exempt). The extracted text was garbled, so check the PDF directly.
12. **Which items the BBPIP list marks as new from 1 March 2026.** They are shown in bold, which did not survive extraction.
13. **Any requirement for a telehealth indicator on the claim** beyond the item number. Not found.
14. **A Services Australia channel for registering enduring agreements.** Not found in the pages reviewed. The Regulations require registration for enduring agreements entered into on or after 1 July 2027 (s 65CB(5)(h)), and registration within 12 months for those made before then (s 65CA(8)(e)).
15. **Privacy (OAIC) requirements specific to telehealth AoB.** Not researched beyond the Department FAQ's reference to the Privacy Act 1988.
