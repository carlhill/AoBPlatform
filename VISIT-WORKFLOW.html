AoB Visit Workflow

A bulk-billed GP visit in an Australian practice, drawn across the four parties that touch the assignment of benefit. Read left to right; time runs with the columns.

The billing gate. The PMS cannot raise a bulk-bill claim unless a patient-approved enduring or episodic agreement is on file for this practitioner on this day. Everything left of the gate exists to get that approval in place before the patient reaches the desk after the consult; everything right of it assumes the approval exists and reconciles against it.

Enduring vs episodic

An enduring agreement stands across visits with one named GP and satisfies the gate on every later visit without a new capture. An episodic agreement covers one practitioner on one day; a patient who sees two providers on the same day needs two. Either one, approved by the patient, opens the gate.

What happens on NO

The PMS shows the status but takes no claim. AoBPlatform re-enters the capture cascade: tablet at the desk, then SMS or email link, then paper, then the assignor pathway. If the patient declines, the practice bills privately and the patient receives a claimable invoice. Verbal assignment is a recorded, risk-flagged fallback only until 30 June 2027.

Who lodges

The PMS always lodges the claim under its own Medicare Online integration. AoBPlatform never talks to Medicare; it reads the appointment book, writes the agreement and its status back into the patient record, and later reads the processing and payment reports to close the loop.

Identity, not the card number

The patient is verified at the desk and on remote links with the RACGP three-point check (name, date of birth, address) against PMS-held values. The Medicare card and IRN are billing particulars, never an identity challenge.
