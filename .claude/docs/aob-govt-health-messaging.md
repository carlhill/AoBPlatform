# Government health messaging — market sizing
### 20 August 2026 · Sizing the compliant sponsored-messaging line (government, not pharma/OTC)

## 1. The market comes in three layers, and only one is open today

| Layer | Size | Who gets it today | Open to us? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal + state health campaign media** | **~$90–120m/yr** (federal health campaigns $61.1m in 2024-25 of a $204.1m whole-of-government total; states add ~$30–60m — estimate) | TV, digital display, out-of-home, press — bought through the Master Media Agency | Effectively no. This is mass-media money; a consent platform is not an ad network the MMA buys |
| **Government-paid patient messaging** | **~$75m/yr contract run-rate** — the National Cancer Screening Register ($445.75m over 6 years, Telstra Health, SMS-first since ~2025), plus a new nationwide childhood-immunisation SMS program from mid-2027 (Budget 2026-27, amount undisclosed, delivered centrally via the AIR) | Central delivery — Telstra Health and Services Australia | Not directly, yet. See §3 |
| **Sponsored messaging through health software** | **~$10–15m/yr** (MedAdvisor's ANZ pharmacy programs are the proxy — ~$25m ANZ revenue, 65 programs across 27 manufacturers, essentially all pharma) | Pharmacy software; pharma-funded | **The government-funded share of this layer today is ~$0.** That is both the gap and the warning |

Reference points: MedAdvisor sold its ANZ business to Jonas Software for $35m (1.4× revenue) in July 2025. Tonic Media Network (GP waiting-room screens, ~3,000 panels) and HealthPoint Digital (~900 pharmacy screens) are advertiser-funded with no published government line. HealthEngine was paid **$3.8m** to build the national COVID vaccine booking platform — the one clean precedent of the federal government paying a health-software vendor to reach patients, and it was for infrastructure, not campaigns.

## 2. The honest sizing for AoBPlatform

The realistic near-term pool is **PHN-commissioned and program-funded patient outreach**: cancer-screening participation campaigns, immunisation catch-up, delivered as GP-endorsed messages through practices. PHNs run ~$2bn/yr of commissioning nationally, but outreach of this kind is funded as small quality-improvement grants, not media buys. Trial-scale precedent exists (the SMARTscreen RCT paid for GP-endorsed SMS to lift bowel screening participation) but there are no published per-message commercial rates because nobody has commercialised it.

**Estimate at scenario-4 scale (~3,300 practices): $1–3m/yr.** A worthwhile ancillary line — comparable to the notice-fee line, smaller than the feedback module — not a pillar. Anyone who sizes this line at $10m+ is assuming a procurement behaviour change that has not happened.

## 3. The two things actually worth doing

**(a) The advocacy play — and it has evidence behind it.** The Government chose *central* AIR delivery for the 2027 immunisation SMS program even though the AuTOMATIC trial (2021-24) showed reminders sent **from the patient's own general practice** produced a ~6% uplift in on-time vaccination over central reminders. A platform holding a verified, trusted, multilingual practice-to-patient channel has a straightforward case to the Department and the PHNs: *practice-channel reminders outperform, and the infrastructure now exists.* That argument costs a submission, not a build.

**(b) Watch the NCSR.** The $445.75m register contract — which includes participant communications — was retendered June–August 2026 for a term from July 2027, with two 3-year extensions. Far too big to bid now; exactly the kind of contract where a proven practice-channel messaging capability becomes a subcontract or a component in the round after. The register's move to SMS-first proves government will pay for digital patient messaging at scale — it just hasn't yet paid for it through the practice channel.

## 4. Why this line is trust-compatible when pharma is not

Government health promotion — immunisation reminders, screening invitations — is the one sponsored-content category that *reinforces* the channel's promise instead of spending it: clearly government-branded, in the patient's interest, no product, no targeting off consultation data. The rules that keep it that way: government or PHN funders only; always branded as such; population-cohort targeting only (age/register-based, never inferred from what the patient consulted about); opt-out honoured; and the consent-request message itself stays inviolate — campaigns ride adjacent surfaces (portal, confirmation messages), never the signature request.
